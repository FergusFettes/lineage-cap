## qN collapse: accurate approximation, not identity

Exact saturation is p=1-exp[m log(1-q)]. Write m=N f: exponent=-f(qN)[1+q/2+q^2/3+...]. Therefore dependence only on qN is exact in the q->0, N->infinity limit, or approximately under rare decisions; at finite N it also depends on q. Replacing qN by -N log(1-q) makes the fixed-N fractional-completion probability collapse exact (except q=1 requires its limiting treatment). For N>=1e8 and qN=1 the maximum absolute event-probability discrepancy from 1-exp(-qN) is 1.8393973e-9; at qN=.01 it is 4.95024e-13; at qN=10 it is 2.2699953e-11. Highly saturated event probabilities are indistinguishable even when exponent corrections are large.

Across all matching saved grid rows with the four abundances and qN<=1e8, the largest relative head-start spread at fixed qN is 1.43324e-5: elapsed planetary visibility, v=.9c, mu=1, qN=1e8, leads .00950010417 to .00950024032 Gyr. This is 0.136 micro-Gyr (136 years), negligible for prose but disproves an exact identity. This comparison also includes finite quadrature/root tolerances; it is not a certified uniform analytic error bound. The statement that curves coincide is numerically useful; 'every bound depends only through qN' should say approximately.

## actual kernels and zero-spread meaning

`expansion.expected_count` integrates **external source galaxies**, homogeneous Poisson positions, with one saturating galaxy event at each distance. It does not count our host, fit a real survey, simulate fronts invading other galaxies, or account for exclusion by earlier expansion. Visibility deadline is now-launch_delay-host_diameter/(v c)-r/c. Arrival deadline is now-launch_delay-r/(v c). Radius is respectively c*(delta-delay) or v c*(delta-launch_delay). Visibility is therefore a light-travel calculation after host crossing, not a v-front survey radius. At .5c the host crossing delay is .195694573 Myr, and a saturated visibility lead corresponds to ~4.15 Mpc light reach, while the arrival lead corresponds to the same ~4.15 Mpc front reach. Andromeda's ~.78 Mpc is substantially closer; 'roughly distance to Andromeda' is a loose comparison.

Inherited clock completions are max(birth,frontier), so zones already born share the frontier and later-born zones complete on birth. The zero-spread bound is a scan over a fastest accessible frontier relative to Earth's observed date. If the additional assertion is that Earth and every existing trajectory have the *identical* inherited deterministic clock and common start/duration, Earth's completion today forces that shared frontier to today: delta=0. An upper permitted frontier gap of 13.7 Myr then remains a conditional diagnostic, not evidence for or a prediction of a literal nonzero shared completion lead. For W>0 the observed Earth completion/common-duration construction consistently identifies delta with our start offset. At W=0 no start ranking exists; `SpreadBound` correctly emits undefined percentiles while preserving the original diagnostic lead.

## observer conditioning: scope assessment

Visibility and arrival refer to the same expansion decisions and cannot be multiplied as independent likelihoods. Arrival does not universally imply visibility in the implemented kernels: for one launch, arrival implies it was already visible only at r>=D/(1-v/c), where D is host diameter. At .5c this is .06 Mpc; nearby arrivals may precede host-wide loudness. Conversely distant light may arrive before the physical front. Thus globally neither event is a subset of the other, although arrival is a subset of visibility outside that small host-delay region under common marks.

Under a fully specified marked-Poisson galaxy model, the elementary relation is P(no V | no A)=exp[-mu(V union A)+mu(A)]=exp[-mu(V\A)]. It requires the joint galaxy marks and overlap, not the two marginal mu values alone. For deterministic clocks with independent per-zone launch marks, the two cumulative eligibility sets can be nested *within each radius*; their union probability can be obtained from the later deadline, enabling a modest joint-kernel extension. Shared-start groups also require joint marginalization over their common starts. This would condition only on no modeled external arrival, not derive observer selection, existence, survival or transport correlations. A complete observer-conditioned likelihood remains beyond the present benchmark. No such assumptions were silently added here.
