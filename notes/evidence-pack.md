# Evidence pack for the lineage-cap paper

Verified 8 October 2026. This is a focused source audit, not a review of abiogenesis. Proposed sentences below separate astronomical observations, transport experiments and the paper's assumptions. None of these sources measures a distribution of lineage start times.

## Hanson: what calibrates which parameter

Source: Hanson et al. (2021), [arXiv abstract and paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.01522), [journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4357/ac2369).

- Supported: their three parameters are birth power, birth constant and expansion speed. The abstract identifies Earth-history hard steps, an assumed uniform distribution of our birth-date rank, and the absence of alien volumes in our sky as their respective calibration inputs.
- Limitation: the rank assumption is substantive; nevertheless the empty sky does inform their model, specifically speed. Calling their birth constant solely an empty-sky residual misstates that division.
- Proposed sentence: “Hanson et al. calibrate the birth power from candidate hard steps in Earth's history, the birth constant through an assumed uniform rank among civilisation birth dates, and the expansion speed from our empty sky.”

## Candidate synchronisers: reach is not a measured start distribution

| Candidate | Source-supported observation | Reach and limitation | Proposed sentence |
|---|---|---|---|
| First stars and enrichment | [Bromm (2013)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.5178) reviews primordial minihalos of roughly a million solar masses collapsing at redshifts 20–30, and early production of ionising photons and heavy elements. | A common cosmological era, with local formation/enrichment histories; not one simultaneous carbon event. Carbon production, dispersal and incorporation into productive chemistry are different dates. The source is a theoretical review, not a measurement of first-carbon start percentiles. | “The first stellar enrichment supplies an early candidate epoch for carbon-based chemistry, but neither its onset nor its regional spread fixes the start distribution of a complexification process.” |
| Reionisation | [Planck Collaboration (2016)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.03507) reconstructs ionisation history; average redshift 7.8–8.8 depends on parameterisation. With kSZ data, one symmetric model gives a width upper limit Δz < 2.8. | A cosmic transition, not a proven single reset of lineage clocks. An upper limit on a model's global redshift width is not a measured lower bound on local timing spread. Do not turn it into a specific Myr exclusion without a cosmology and a local mechanism. | “Reionisation is another cosmic transition, although no mechanism here establishes that it resets complexification clocks together.” |
| Cosmic noon | [Madau & Dickinson (2014)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.0007) give a global star-formation-rate peak about 3.5 Gyr after the Big Bang (z ≈ 1.9), followed by a 3.9 Gyr decline e-folding time. | Broad statistical cosmic history; a peak is not a simultaneous trigger for individual galaxies. The e-folding time is not a uniform window width W. This is a data synthesis/review. | “The cosmic star-formation peak is a broad population history rather than a demonstrated simultaneous trigger.” |
| Sagittarius-associated episodes | [Ruiz-Lara et al. (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.12577), [journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41550-020-1097-0), [full author manuscript](https://openaccess.inaf.it/server/api/core/bitstreams/8c6bd0d1-3c79-4abb-9358-58ebc7d274e1/content), main text following Fig. 2. Episodes around 5.7, 1.9 and 1.0 Gyr ago have fitted durations 0.8, 0.2 and 0.1 Gyr, defined as twice Gaussian sigma. | Observations reconstruct a roughly 2-kpc-radius bubble around the Sun. Association with proposed Sagittarius passages is suggestive. These are neither measured whole-disk synchronous onsets nor lineage reset dates. | “Ruiz-Lara et al. reconstruct three enhanced star-formation episodes in the approximately 2-kpc neighbourhood of the Sun, associated with proposed Sagittarius passages; this does not establish intergalactic synchrony.” |
| Solar birth aggregate | [Adams (2010)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1001.5444) reviews constrained possible birth environments, explicitly retaining substantial uncertainty. [Adams & Napier (2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07799) model enhanced rock exchange in a birth cluster. | A local shared environment and material-transfer opportunity, not a measured common biological start. Cluster lifetime, star-formation spread, transfer time and lineage start spread must not be substituted for one another. “Thousands of siblings” should be qualified as an inferred scenario, not observed membership. | “A solar birth cluster could provide local shared material and enhanced rock exchange, but cannot by itself establish common start dates across galaxies.” |

The reach assessment is an inference about candidate mechanisms, not a proof that first carbon is unique. Shared cosmic conditions need not involve shared ancestry. Conversely, inheritance needs successful transfer and retention, not merely a shared calendar epoch. The categorical claim that matter in different galaxies never subsequently shares trajectories is unsupported here and should be removed; these sources establish no such prohibition.

## The illustrative 100–200 Myr window

Bromm's redshift range is not a first-carbon probability distribution. A redshift-to-age conversion additionally requires an explicit cosmology; adjoining a later redshift does not make the interval a measured distribution. The baseline's 0.2 Gyr epoch and uniform full width W are scenario inputs. A 100–200 Myr sensitivity interval can be retained, labelled illustrative. Its inferred leading-start percentiles are conditional model outputs and do not predict an observed Milky Way progenitor rank without identifying the relevant ancestry groups and their true start distribution.

Proposed sentence: “We use uniform 100–200 Myr start windows as illustrative sensitivity cases, not as measured first-carbon distributions; their permitted percentiles describe this assumed window.”

## Transport: four distinct evidential levels

1. **Organic ingredients.** [NASA's Bennu sample report (29 January 2025)](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasas-asteroid-bennu-sample-reveals-mix-of-lifes-ingredients/) describes 14 protein-building amino acids and all five DNA/RNA nucleobases in returned material. It explicitly reports no evidence of life. [Underlying Nature Astronomy paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41550-024-02472-9). These molecules are neither an intact genome nor retained functional hereditary information. Proposed sentence: “Bennu samples demonstrate extraterrestrial availability of biological building blocks, rather than transported life or a continuous hereditary lineage.”

2. **Viable microbes under specified exposure.** [Kawaguchi et al. (2020), Tanpopo](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/microbiology/articles/10.3389/fmicb.2020.02050/full), abstract and discussion: dried 500-μm-thick Deinococcus pellets retained viable cells after three years outside the ISS; repair occurred on cultivation. Several-year interplanetary survival estimates extrapolate exposure curves and account for illumination. This does not test natural ejection, impact, establishment, or interstellar/Gyr transit. Proposed sentence: “Tanpopo demonstrates survival of protected microbial aggregates during three years of low-Earth-orbit exposure, leaving longer transport and successful establishment unresolved.”

3. **Functional DNA after short atmospheric transit.** [Thiel et al. (2014)](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0112979), abstract and discussion: artificial plasmid DNA attached to a sounding rocket remained partly recoverable and functional in transformation/transfection assays after a 780-second flight, with about 400 seconds above 100 km. Up to 35% retained assay-defined function. This is not organism survival or sustained autonomous replication, and provides no Myr–Gyr stability test. Proposed sentence: “A sounding-rocket experiment recovered functional plasmid DNA after short atmospheric passage, demonstrating one component of transfer rather than long-term lineage continuity.”

4. **Successful seeding.** [Adams & Napier (2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07799), [journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1089/ast.2021.0187): rock transfer is enhanced in birth clusters, yet their adopted inputs yield low odds of successful panspermia; the expected inventory of alien rocks can still be substantial. Biological development times and establishment efficiency remain uncertain. This is a conditional dynamical/probability calculation, not an observed seeding event or a universal impossibility proof. Proposed sentence: “Dynamical capture of interstellar rocks does not establish biological seeding: Adams and Napier find enhanced birth-cluster transfer but low success probabilities under their adopted biological assumptions.”

The paper's strong transport assumption is inherited continuity across repeated transfer, retention and productive accumulation over cosmic time. None of these experiments or calculations establishes that chain. Organic delivery, viable exposure, DNA functionality and successful seeding should retain separate names and evidential status.
