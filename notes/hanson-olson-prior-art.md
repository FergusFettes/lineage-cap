# Hanson, Olson, and the lineage-clock proposal

Research note, 12 October 2026. The paper itself was not changed for this search.

## Assessment

There is substantial prior discussion of the ingredients: pre-Earth ancestry,
slow but comparatively reliable evolution, astrophysical synchronisation,
expanding civilisations, and anthropic timing. I did not find the exact combined
argument developed in this project: assume common productive lineages, ask how
little earlier the frontier can have completed given an empty sky, then compare
planetary starts with inherited progress and a common early cosmic onset.
That is a bounded search result, not a claim to have established novelty.

The strongest positioning is a quantitative comparison of completion clocks
within established expansion/visibility reasoning. Neither panspermia nor
cosmic synchronisation nor empty-sky light-cone constraints should be presented
as new discoveries here. The biological mechanism connecting accumulated
opportunity to a completion frontier remains a separate hypothesis.

## Closest Hanson precedents

- **1998, [Must Early Life Be Easy? The Rhythm of Major Evolutionary Transitions](https://hanson.gmu.edu/hardstep.pdf).**
  Develops the conditional hard-step timing argument. On printed pp. 8–9,
  considers one early critical step followed by steady easy advances, with
  intelligence potentially becoming almost assured after four to ten billion
  years. Also allows an environmental oxygen delay. This is an explicit
  precedent for long development without making every transition a rare
  lottery; it retains a potentially rare origin and does not supply our cosmic
  onset or frontier inference.
- **2009-11-03, [Pondering Panspermia](https://www.overcomingbias.com/p/pondering-panspermiahtml).**
  Hanson says he came to favour an origin outside our solar system. Discusses
  transfer through stellar nurseries and molecular clouds, among other routes.
  We should not frame his position as necessarily Earth-bound abiogenesis.
- **2010-11-28, [At Least Two Filters](https://www.overcomingbias.com/p/at-least-two-filtershtml).**
  Directly asks why easy spread of simple life, followed by easy evolution,
  has not produced visible predecessors. Considers being first possible but
  unlikely by default. This is the clearest earlier statement of the objection
  our common-clock proposal tries to answer.
- **2012-10-25, [Two Kinds of Panspermia](https://www.overcomingbias.com/p/two-kinds-of-panspermiahtml).**
  Distinguishes growth in harsh space environments from growth in favourable
  habitats interrupted by decline during transport. The latter already allows
  continuity across habitats without requiring continuous active evolution in
  interstellar space.
- **2015-03-13, [Life Before Earth](https://www.overcomingbias.com/p/life-before-earthhtml).**
  Discusses Sharov and Gordon's genome extrapolation, a proposed origin roughly
  ten billion years ago, and a long route to intelligence. Hanson finds its
  proximity to the universe's age supportive of pre-Earth origins. The numerical
  claims in the block quote belong to Sharov and Gordon, not a calculation by
  Hanson. His endorsement is qualified, and this post does not establish the
  extrapolation's validity.
- **2021-05-20, [Panspermia Siblings](https://www.overcomingbias.com/p/panspermia-siblingshtml).**
  Considers shared nursery ancestry, substantial delay steps, and differences
  of hundreds of millions of years in the arrival of advanced sibling species.
  However, this is a speculative UFO scenario retaining hard steps, a rare
  ancestral source, and long-lived non-expanding civilisations. It is neither
  universal synchronisation nor the present model.
- **2024-08-27, updated 2025-02-21, [Life Without Hard Steps?](https://www.overcomingbias.com/p/life-without-hard-steps).**
  Distinguishes enabling conditions, fixed delays, repeated rare attempts, and
  one-shot outcomes. Grants that known biological transitions could involve
  enabling conditions or delays. Challenges the no-hard-steps alternative to
  explain the empty sky and the timing of transitions relative to Earth's
  remaining habitability. Our proposed recent first flush addresses his sky
  objection conditionally; it does not yet establish comparable anthropic
  likelihoods or explain every Earth-history timing observation.

The [2021 grabby-aliens paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2102.01522v3), especially
sections 3–6, explicitly allows delay/easy/try-once ingredients and discusses
pre-Earth hard steps and panspermia, citing Sharov and Gordon. Its detailed
planet-habitability timing calculation nevertheless adopts no panspermia.
It is fair to contrast our completion law with its power-law appearance law;
it would be inaccurate to say its authors overlooked inherited ancestry.

The [project website](https://grabbyaliens.com/) assigns different inferential
roles to the three model parameters: the hard-step exponent comes from
evolutionary timing, the overall time scale from our assumed appearance rank,
and expansion speed from non-observation. “Tuning the chance of going grabby
to match an empty sky” loses these distinctions.

## The seven Olson links, in the website's order

Years below identify first preprints; journal publication can be later.

| Link | Paper | Contribution and relevance |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Homogeneous cosmology with aggressively expanding civilizations](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.4359), 2014 preprint / 2015 journal | Cosmological expansion framework with competing strategies and a delay between arrival and visible transformation. Its illustrative appearance history uses planets aged 4.5–6 Gyr. Strong mathematical precedent; not committed to hard-step lotteries. |
| 2 | [Estimates for the number of visible galaxy-spanning civilizations and the cosmological expansion of life](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.05969), 2015 | Uses our unoccupied location and assumptions about our arrival-time typicality to constrain rates and observable distances. |
| 3 | [On the visible size and geometry of aggressively expanding civilizations at cosmological distances](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.01521), 2015 | Computes apparent shapes, angular sizes, and sky coverage. Important if we extend host-galaxy visibility to expanding domains. |
| 4 | [Long-term implications of observing an expanding cosmological civilization](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.07522), 2016 | First-mover advantage, boundaries between expanding domains, and restrictions on our future reach. Less directly about the origin clock. |
| 5 | [Life-hostile conditions in the early universe can increase the present-day odds of observing extragalactic life](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.04125), 2017 | Removing early opportunities can increase inferred present visibility when the rate is recalibrated to preserve our typicality and expansion suppresses later origins. This is conditional on that inference procedure, not a universal theorem against delayed onset. |
| 6 | [Expanding cosmological civilizations on the back of an envelope](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.06329), 2018 | Compact formulas relating appearance history, expansion speed, occupation, and visibility. The clearest starting point for comparing our calculation to established machinery. |
| 7 | [On the Likelihood of Observing Extragalactic Civilizations: Predictions from the Self-Indication Assumption](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08194), 2020 | Makes the anthropic school explicit, derives predictions, and updates them with survey outcomes. Under broad log-uniform rate priors and its other assumptions, some visibility predictions lose dependence on the detailed appearance history. |

Full-text checks concentrated on 1, 5, 6, and 7; the remaining entries were
screened using their abstracts and available text. This is a relevance survey,
not an independent verification of every derivation in seven papers.

A particularly relevant later paper is Olson and Ord,
[Implications of a search for intergalactic civilizations on prior estimates of human survival and travel speed](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.13348)
(2021). It separates the human-stage appearance rate, the fraction that expands,
and expansion speed, and shows how SIA plus survey results updates beliefs.
It also explicitly acknowledges panspermia-based alternative appearance
histories. It supplies a concrete comparison for claims about the anthropic
cost of a small expansion fraction versus an early appearance.

## Synchronisation and no-hard-steps work elsewhere

- **Annis (1999), [An Astrophysical Explanation for the Great Silence](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9901322).**
  Proposes that a declining gamma-ray-burst hazard permits a transition to
  widespread intelligence. Explicitly discusses near-contemporaneous emergence
  across a galaxy. This is prior art for astrophysical synchronisation, with a
  different mechanism from early onset plus a long inherited clock.
- **Ćirković and Vukotić (2008), [Astrobiological phase transition](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18855114/), and Ćirković, Vukotić and Dragićević (2009), [Galactic punctuated equilibrium](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19566428/).**
  Develop global regulation and correlated evolutionary opportunities as
  challenges to Carter-style timing arguments. Their galaxy-scale focus leaves
  the required intergalactic reach and timing spread to be assessed separately.
- **Sharov and Gordon (2013), [Life Before Earth](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.3381).**
  Direct precedent for an ancient lineage inferred from extrapolated genetic
  complexity. Our timing constraint should stand independently of accepting
  their extrapolation.
- **Mills, Macalady, Frank and Wright (2025), [A reassessment of the “hard-steps” model for the evolution of intelligent life](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11827626/).**
  Explores sequential environmental opportunities as an alternative to intrinsic
  improbability. Its Earth-system clock differs from a pre-Earth inherited
  lineage; Hanson's 2024/2025 response identifies the empty-sky question to
  which our comparison is directed.

## Consequences for the paper: interpretation, not edits

1. **Credit the geometry.** Cite Olson for the arrival/visibility distinction,
   light-cone counting, and cosmological extension. Our present model counts
   persistently loud host galaxies; it does not reproduce his full domain
   geometry, overlap, or displacement of later origins.
2. **Describe the alternative precisely.** Compare a rare-completion
   appearance law with common lineages whose completion times are concentrated
   by inherited progress and shared timing. Slow development and inheritance
   are already admitted as ingredients in the earlier literature.
3. **Keep likelihoods distinct from anthropic conclusions.** Our
   `P(empty | parameters)` and allowed start percentile are not an
   observer-conditioned model comparison. Equal dependence on anthropic
   reasoning does not establish equal numerical plausibility. A future
   comparison needs the same reference class, priors, and occupation model on
   both sides. Olson 2017 and 2020 demonstrate that the choice can matter.
4. **Separate onset from duration.** For an assumed common onset, a near-present
   completion yields a long duration by subtraction. Empty-sky constraints do
   not themselves identify first carbon as the unique onset event.
5. **Make the potential contribution narrow and useful.** Quantify the permitted
   completion lead and finite-spread rank under stated abundance and visibility
   assumptions, then show what changes when the clock follows ancestry rather
   than the receiving planet's birth.

## Follow-up found while checking duration variance

The next search located Oleg Ponfilenok's GitHub manuscript,
[The Critical Path of Evolution](https://github.com/oponfil/critical-path-of-evolution/blob/main/critical_path_evolution_en.tex).
It is a substantially closer conceptual overlap than the sources above:
many easy steps, a cosmic-age developmental path, common cosmic timing,
and a dispersion bound inferred from the absence of loud civilisations.
It uses a CMB thermal gate and an independent-increment concentration argument.
This discovery narrows the earlier novelty assessment. Repository availability
does not establish its publication status, priority, or validity, and its
specific quantitative and physical claims have not been audited here.
The mathematical concentration argument alone does not establish that
different lineages share the same mean rate or that environmental variation
is negligible. The new duration-spread calculation in this project treats
dispersion and tail shape as explicit sensitivities instead.
