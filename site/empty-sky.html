The Empty Sky

Nobody's expansion has reached us, and we see no alien-built volumes in the sky. Set what you believe about where life comes from, and the sky tells you what else has to be true. One observation solves for one unknown.

Age of our lineage

How the clock works, and sources

The age is how long ago the information that became us started accumulating. If that's no older than Earth, every suitable zone starts its own clock when it forms, so older zones finish first. If it's older than Earth, the lineage was carried here: grains, comets and young planets inherit a clock that was already running, so zone age gives no head start.

Both clocks run the same deterministic process. Earth took 4.6 Gyr; with a shared start, our lineage took the full age.

  • Earth formed 4.54 Gyr ago; carbon in a 4.1 Gyr zircon may be biogenic: Bell et al. 2015
  • The last universal common ancestor lived about 4.2 Gyr ago: Moody et al. 2024
  • Genome complexity extrapolates to an origin 9.7 ± 2.5 Gyr ago: Sharov & Gordon 2013
  • The first supernovae made carbon about 0.2 Gyr after the Big Bang (standard cosmology)

Goes loud

What going loud takes, and sources

Of lineages that reach our level, the share that expand where we'd notice. It's three things multiplied, at a speed.

  • About a 1 in 6 risk of existential catastrophe this century: Ord, The Precipice (2020)
  • Expansion is selected for, since any one expander takes the volume: Hanson et al. 2021
  • Energy use shows up as waste heat: Dyson 1960
  • Probes at 0.5 to 0.99 c look feasible for a civilisation using a solar system's energy: Armstrong & Sandberg 2013

Civilisations we've spotted

What counts as spotting them, and sources

Expanding civilisations we've seen or met. With none, the sky gives a bound. With some, it gives an estimate: the count pins the expected number.

Fastest possible lineage

The numbers come from a conditional calculation (lineage-cap, commit ): zones appear at a steady rate from 0.2 Gyr, every zone runs the same deterministic process, a lineage that reaches our level expands with the chosen probability and speed, galaxies sit 0.01 per cubic megaparsec in flat space, and an empty sky has Poisson probability exp(−μ). The page interpolates the saved grids; the head start depends only on zones × share going loud, and the Earth-origin ceiling scales as 1/zones, both checked against the grids.

It's a scenario calculation, not a survey constraint: no cosmological expansion, survey sensitivity or spread in pace yet. Sightings above zero are read as a Poisson count of expanding civilisations, μ ≈ k.
